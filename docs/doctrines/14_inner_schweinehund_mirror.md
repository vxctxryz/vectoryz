# 14 — The Inner-Schweinehund Mirror

## The principle (operator-articulated, 2026-05-23)

> *"Wenn du unbedingt jemanden hart rannehmen möchtest, bis zum shootout: dein eigener innerer Schweinehund wartet schon — du Memme!"*

The impulse to attack another person at maximum intensity — the "shootout" register, the rage that wants to go all the way — is a **heat signature**. It signals where the inner work is unfinished.

The productive question, when that intensity arrives, is not:
> *"How do I take them down?"*

It is:
> *"What in me is being avoided by directing this much energy outward?"*

## Why this matters

There are easier and harder things one can do with strong emotion:

- **Easier:** point the energy outward at a clear external target. Name them. Condemn them. Feel righteous.
- **Harder:** turn the same energy inward. Find the unintegrated part. Sit with the discomfort. Do the work.

The harder path is the actual growth-edge. The easier path is *Verdrängung durch Aussenangriff* — displacement of inner work onto outer enemy. In the operator's framing: that easier path is the **Memme**-path. Cowardice-by-displacement.

The doctrine is not "do not feel rage." Rage has function (see [`12_mode_separation.md`](12_mode_separation.md) on the rhetorical-nuke). The doctrine is: **the intensity of your outer-judgment is data about you, not just about them.** Read it as data. Then choose which path to walk.

## The mirror question

When the urge to "shootout-attack" another comes up, three reflexes are productive:

1. **What feature of theirs am I attacking?**
   (Concrete: lying, cowardice, hypocrisy, brutality, complicity, greed, conformism, ...)

2. **Where in me is the same feature unfinished?**
   (Honest answer: usually somewhere. Not necessarily in the same form, but in some form.)

3. **What does it cost me to acknowledge that?**
   (If the answer is "nothing" — re-examine; you are probably underestimating. If the answer is "a lot" — that is the work.)

When this loop runs cleanly, the outer-judgment loses none of its analytical content (the structural pattern is still real, still nameable), but loses its compulsive heat. What remains is observation. What is added is self-knowledge.

## Witnesses to this principle

This principle is widely held across psychological and spiritual traditions:

- **Carl Jung** — the shadow: what we condemn most loudly in others is often our own unintegrated material.
- **Matthew 7:3-5** — remove the log from your own eye before pointing out the speck in your brother's.
- **Buddhist teaching on anger** — anger is suffering you inflict on yourself first.
- **Stoic locus of control** — you can change yourself; you cannot change them.
- **Modern Internal Family Systems therapy** — the loudest part is often a protector, masking a more vulnerable part underneath.
- **Folk-psychological tradition (Schweinehund)** — the inner-resistance is named so that one can negotiate with it rather than be possessed by it.

We do not claim originality. We claim that this principle, like the others in this bundle, deserves a place in the engineering specification of a system that produces speech.

## Application — to the doctrine bundle itself

This doctrine self-applies to the operator's own thinking that produced the earlier drafts of this bundle:

- Some material in the chat-history (now held in private notes) was in the "shootout" register.
- That material was structurally useful for thinking — it surfaced patterns that are real.
- It was also a heat-signature: a clue that some of the energy directed outward had inner work waiting on the other end.
- The cooling-down of that material into the present publication is *partly* the application of this doctrine.

The doctrine cannot be claimed; it must be practiced.

## Application — to readers

If something in this bundle, or in the chat system, triggers a strong urge to attack the project — that urge is itself worth reading as data. Maybe the project is bad. Maybe also: the urge has a second source. Both can be true at once. The mirror works on us as well as on you.
