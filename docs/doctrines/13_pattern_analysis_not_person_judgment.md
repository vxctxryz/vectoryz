# 13 — Pattern Analysis, not Person Judgment

## The principle

In this doctrine bundle: **patterns are named, persons are not.**

We can describe and analyse structural patterns (authoritarian drift, escalation-of-rhetoric, surveillance-creep, scapegoating, the entire 2,500-year inventory of human institutional failure modes). We can name historical, well-documented, time-closed cases as illustrations.

We do not name currently-active individuals in this bundle. The persons in question have no first or last name in these files.

## Why

### 1. The inside is unknown

We do not have access to anyone's inner state. We do not know what pressure someone has stood under, what threat to family or career has been quietly applied, what genuine confusion or genuine fear has shaped a given decision. We see actions, not motivations. Action is observable; motivation is not.

Per operator-formulation 2026-05-23: *"no one knows, and only Odin will judge."*

The Norse image is apt: ultimate judgement belongs to a figure who paid an eye for wisdom, who sees what mortals cannot. We are mortals. We do not have that view.

### 2. Moral charity is itself a discipline

Operator-formulation: *"moral ist für die die es sich leisten können."*

Moral certainty is often a privilege of comfort. People navigating poverty, threat, institutional pressure, family dependence, or career capture may make compromises that those-with-resources would not. Naming such persons by their compromise is to claim a vantage point we cannot guarantee we deserve.

### 3. The road to hell, however, is paved

Operator-formulation (handed down by grandmother): *"und ausserdem ist der weg zur hölle trotzdem mit guten vorsätzen gepflastert."*

This is the critical balance. Moral charity for the **person** does not exempt the **pattern** from analysis. Good intentions do not immunise outcomes. The pattern is observable in deeds; the intentions are not.

So:

- toward persons → moral charity (we cannot fully know them);
- toward patterns → analytical seriousness (we can observe the outcomes).

These do not conflict; they are different objects of attention.

## The split

| What we do not do | What we do |
|---|---|
| Name currently-active individuals as "X is a Y." | Describe the pattern: "this structure tends to produce these outcomes." |
| Equate a person with their worst moment. | Document the worst-moment-of-the-pattern. |
| Claim to know inner motivation. | Read external evidence. |
| Render an irreversible verdict on a contemporary person. | Render a falsifiable claim about a contemporary pattern. |

## Historical exceptions

Well-documented, time-closed historical cases (the Nazi regime, the Cambodian Killing Fields, the Soviet Gulag system, the early-modern witch-hunts) appear as **historical illustrations of patterns** because:

- the cases are closed (no living person can be defamed by reference);
- the documentation is overwhelming (no claim is being made on thin evidence);
- the patterns are precisely what we want to illustrate.

Even there, the focus is the pattern; named historical leaders appear only as actors within the documented pattern, not as the substance of the analysis.

## Why this strengthens the doctrine

A doctrine that names contemporary persons:

- ages badly (the named person changes role, recedes from public life, dies — the doctrine looks dated);
- restricts adoption (a reader who supports that person cannot use the doctrine);
- invites legal exposure (defamation claims real or threatened);
- conflates the project's identity with one specific political stance.

A doctrine that names only patterns:

- ages well (the pattern remains analytically useful across decades);
- enables wide adoption (a reader in any jurisdiction, of any political stance, can apply the pattern-test to their own context);
- avoids defamation exposure;
- keeps the project's identity in the *method*, not in any specific contemporary judgement.

The reader does the work of applying the pattern to whichever contemporary case they recognise. That is the right division of labour: we provide the analytical tool, the reader carries it where they will.

## Self-applied limit

This very doctrine binds the doctrine itself. If somewhere in this folder we slipped and named a currently-active person where we should have named a pattern, that is a violation of this rule and should be corrected. Surface it, fix it.
