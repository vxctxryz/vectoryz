# 12 — Mode Separation: Chat vs. Publication

## The principle

There is a meaningful difference between two modes of speaking:

- **Hot-head mode** — the chat-internal, exploratory, principle-probing mode. Sometimes uses explicit-over-exaggerated language, the "Greek-idiot-idea" rhetorical posture (Socratic provocation through overstatement). Stereotypes, unfinished thoughts, cathartic vents are allowed because they serve the *thinking process*.

- **Publication mode** — the cooled-down, public-ready mode. Sharpness of formulation precedes sharpness of accusation. Stereotypes are audited. Evidence is stabilised. All sides are treated fairly. This is what is fit for a permanent, MIT-licensed, world-readable document.

Both modes are legitimate. They serve different functions. **They must not be confused.**

## Why the distinction matters

What functions as catharsis in conversation can function as defamation in publication.
What sharpens an internal thought-process can repel an external good-faith reader.
What is a rhetorical attention-nuke in a heated debate is empty noise on a project page.

The transition from one mode to the other requires conscious cooling-down work.

## The cooling-down discipline

When moving from chat to publication, four checks apply:

1. **Stereotype audit:** does the text contain stereotypes I would not hold if I were calm?
2. **Belegt-genug check:** can I defend this statement in a hostile-but-good-faith inquiry — and do I have to?
3. **Sharpness check:** is the formulation sharp (precise) or just loud?
4. **Fair-to-other-side check:** would a thoughtful opponent say "yes, this argument I must take seriously", or would they say "this is just hating on me"?

A claim passing all four checks is publication-ready. A claim failing any of them stays in the chat-history.

## The rhetorical-nuke function

Strong language ("Scheiß...", "Drecks...", "verrecket", and so on) in hot-head mode serves an attention function. It is a megaphone. It signals: *this matters, listen, the stakes are real*.

This is legitimate rhetorical practice — **if and only if** the megaphone is followed by substantive discussion. The nuke gets people to listen; the substance is what the listening was for.

- without substance: empty rage. "grrrr!!"
- with substance: rhetoric → discussion → enlightening (irie + ovastandin).

The transition from chat-banter to publication usually drops the nuke entirely, because the substance now stands on its own and the publication-mode reader does not need an attention-grab; they came to read.

## Anti-clash discipline

The goal of publication is **discussion and enlightening**, not clash. Clash repels conversation partners. Enlightening invites them.

A reader who feels personally attacked by a doctrine document is a reader lost to the conversation. A reader who feels invited into a thought-process is a reader gained.

Per operator-instruction 2026-05-23: *"we want discussion and enlightening, antiClash."* Strong language in publication risks tipping the document into clash-mode and losing readers who would otherwise have engaged.

## Politics and religion: same hot field

Both political and religious topics are emotionally loaded, identity-loaded, defamation-sensitive, clash-prone. They require the same cooling-down discipline. They are not exceptions.

## Self-application

This very document — and the doctrine bundle around it — has been cooled down from substantial hot-head-mode chat thinking. Some of the original thinking was sharper, more named, more accusatory. That sharper thinking remains in the operator's private chat-history. The publication is the cooled-down distillate. By design.
