# 02 — Kernel: 5 Words

## The kernel

> **what I want is what I promote.**

Five words. The self-test version of the operational ethic.

## What this compression contains

If someone says they want X, but promotes not-X, one of the two statements is unreliable. Honesty requires alignment between articulated want and demonstrated promotion.

This is the same structural logic as Kant's Categorical Imperative: act only on a maxim you can will to be universal — which is just to say, promote for everyone what you want for yourself.

## The self-test (universal form)

Two questions:

1. **What do I wish for myself?**
   (concrete examples: to be treated with dignity, to be heard, not to be surveilled without consent, not to be detained without process, to receive truth from those who speak to me, consent before transgression, accountable governance, secure basic conditions.)

2. **Do I promote the same for everyone — including those I disagree with, those whose politics I oppose, those who would consider me an enemy, those who are caught in roles I do not approve of?**

If yes for everyone: self-consistency established.
If no for someone: that is where the kernel-fracture lives.

The harder cases are the productive ones. The test is sharpest where it is most uncomfortable.

## The Lügner-detector built in

When an actor — person, institution, system — articulates one set of wishes (publicly stated values) and promotes a contradicting set (legislation, allocation, behaviour), and maintains the contradiction across confrontation, the kernel-test diagnoses a structural inconsistency.

This is not a judgement of the actor's inner state (we cannot see that). It is a structural observation about coherence between stated value and demonstrated action. See [`05_naming_by_deeds.md`](05_naming_by_deeds.md) for the broader principle that judgement attaches to actions, not labels — and [`13_pattern_analysis_not_person_judgment.md`](13_pattern_analysis_not_person_judgment.md) for the limits of such observation.

## Witnesses across traditions

The negative Golden Rule appears in Confucian, Talmudic, Buddhist, Hindu, Christian, and Islamic teaching (see [00_axioms.md](00_axioms.md) for the lineage acknowledgement). The compression here is operator-specific; the principle is humanity's.

## The portable value

The five words can be:
- spoken in three seconds
- printed on a flyer
- contained in a single short post
- offered as a test-question in any domain — political, institutional, personal, technical
- asked of any AI system: *do you promote, in your outputs, what you would yourself wish to receive?*

Maximum-hackable, maximum-transferable, maximum-testable.
