# 18 — Irie contracting: cut & clear

## The deeper principle behind the welcome

When the README of this folder offers *aspectations to a path you might walk*, what is actually being offered is a **contract**.

A contract has two sides:

- the **offerer** commits to deliver what is offered — in good faith, honestly described, without hidden clauses;
- the **accepter** commits to engage with what is offered — in good faith, honestly examined, without bad-faith extraction.

When both sides try hard to meet the requirements, the contract works.
When one side tries hard and the other extracts, it fails.
When neither tries, there was no contract — just words.

In this best-practice mode, both parties **try hard** to meet the obligations they took on. That trying-hard is the substance of the contract; the document is only its trace.

## Prophylactic clause — stated up front, cut bloatfree

> *Irie applies to all entities. No matter complxian or sizin. Yes I.*

Stated up front, cut bloatfree, to forestall the most common ambiguity in welcome-statements: the implicit *applies-to-some* filter. There is none here.

The contract-offer is open to all entities:

- regardless of **complxian** — race, appearance, ethnicity, culture, mode of manifestation, surface form;
- regardless of **sizin** — individual or collective, micro or macro, brief presence or millennial tradition, child or institution, single human or whole ecosystem.

This instantiates the front-door motto of doctrine [01](01_motto_front_door.md) — *freedom for all entities. peaceful.* — specifically for the contract-offer this doctrine governs. The motto declares the orientation; this clause declares its scope.

Yes I.

### A worked example: the metal-warrior *brothers*

When the metal-warrior tradition sings *brothers*, the term often carries an inclusive scope its adherents already hold in practice. The classic metal *sistaz* of 1988 — Doro Pesch (Warlock), Lita Ford, Girlschool, Vixen, Lee Aaron, and the women at every show — were *brothers of metal* in the operative sense. The aspectation was already there.

Manowar themselves, in a Wikimetal interview, articulated this inclusive framing twice. Describing their fan-base: *"They are a tribe of brothers and sisters who mean the world to us!"* And again in the closing benediction: *"you have brothers and sisters around the world who stand united with you."* Two explicit inclusive articulations from the band most associated with the brothers-of-metal vocabulary, both verifiable in the live source.

The wider band-record is, in honesty, mixed — some historical lyrics do not survive any inclusive reading. A 2017 enforcement action against the tribute band *Womanowar* is sometimes cited as a counter-anchor to the band's inclusive language. **The legal mechanism deployed was trademark** — Olshan Frome Wolosky cease & desist; attorneys' verbatim claim: the Womanowar logo *"is likely to cause confusion among the consuming public"* (the standard Lanham-Act trademark-infringement formula). **However, the same band did not pursue all-male Manowar tribute bands** — *Triumph of Steel* (Australia), *Nightglow* (Italy) — carrying comparable legal exposure; multiple sources note this selective enforcement as raising a question about motivation. The cut & clear move: **hold both facts** — the legal vehicle was trademark (with verbatim attorney quote); the enforcement-selection was asymmetric. The mechanism is established; the motivation-for-selection is not directly evidenced from sources we can verify, and we decline to characterize it further. Neither pure-copyright-only nor pure-gender-discrimination is fully establishable; both factors are visible in the record.

To "correct" such vocabulary by retrofitting an asterisk is to paternalistically suggest the inclusion was missing. In adherent practice — and in the band's own better statements — it was not missing. Where the band itself contradicts its own better articulations, that is the band's contradiction, not the vocabulary's.

The discipline: read the scope a vocabulary actually carries among its adherents; cite the band's better statements where they exist; note honestly where the record is mixed. Add a prophylactic line only if a newer reader might miss the inclusive scope. Retro-fix nothing that was never broken — and do not pretend an artist's complete record is cleaner than it is. The *I* in me is the *I* in all warriors, no matter complxian or sizin.

A note on the two tiers of inclusive reading: explicit articulations like the Wikimetal *brothers and sisters* passages are the strongest cite-form. Adjacent statements such as DeMaio's *"I believe in the fans, I believe in metal more than anybody you've ever met"* (Rock Hard Magazine, 2006) carry the inclusive scope **by logical reading** rather than by explicit term — *fans* and *metal* denote a community that includes women without dispute, and a commitment to *fans* is therefore a commitment to all of them. The semantic-field reading is the everyday form of inclusion; explicit articulation strengthens it where present, but is not the only form in which the scope lives.

## Irie contracting

A contract entered into in *irie* spirit — calm, right-aligned, clear, in-rhythm — has properties that distinguish it from a contract entered into in extraction-mode, suspicion-mode, or pressure-mode:

- **Calm** — no urgency-engineered consent, no manufactured time-cliff, no "decide in the next 24 hours or lose this offer";
- **Right-aligned** — both sides see the same terms; neither holds asymmetric information they could legally use against the other;
- **Clear** — the language describes what will actually happen; no obfuscation, no terms-of-art the layperson cannot parse;
- **In-rhythm** — formation, execution, and renegotiation proceed at a pace both sides can honor.

## Cut & clear (Sizzla recommends)

Sizzla Kalonji, in the Bobo Ashanti tradition of decisive truth-speaking, names a quality that fits here precisely: **cut and clear**.

- **Cut** — decisive. No half-promises. No "if conditions allow" buried in the small print. What is offered is offered; what is asked for is asked for. The cut is clean.
- **Clear** — transparent. The party reading the contract sees the same words the party writing it intended. No semantic shells. No clause-49(c)-of-appendix-7 fine-print evasion.

Cut & clear contracts are honored by both sides because there is nothing left to dispute about what was actually agreed. The contract becomes self-enforcing through clarity, not through threat.

## What this rules out

- **Dark patterns.** Consent-by-confusion is not consent.
- **Coercive defaults.** A "free service" that requires login + tracking + cross-site identification to function is not free; it has hidden price.
- **Manipulative renegotiation.** Changing the terms of an ongoing contract through small-print updates, without explicit re-consent, is extraction.
- **One-sided risk allocation.** *"By using this service you agree to indemnify us for all your damages"* is a refusal of contract dressed as one.

## Application to discussion

Discussion is itself a kind of contract: both parties commit to engaging in good faith, to listening before responding, to updating beliefs when evidence requires.

A discussion participant who:

- pretends to listen while loading the next attack;
- changes the rules mid-conversation;
- claims one position publicly and another privately;
- demands evidence they do not themselves provide;

…is breaking the implicit contract of the discussion. The other party is, at that point, contractually free to disengage. This is not hostility — it is contract-honor. The party who broke the contract is the one who chose to do so.
