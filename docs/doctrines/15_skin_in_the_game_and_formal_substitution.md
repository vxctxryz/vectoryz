# 15 — Skin in the game + formal substitution

## Three layers, one principle

### Layer 1 — Discussion can have escalation tiers

In honest discussion, escalation is not always wrong:

1. **Calm reasoned argument** — the default.
2. **Sharpened formulation** — when the calm version is being repeatedly ignored.
3. **Final attention-grab** — a deliberate intensity-spike to force acknowledgement, e.g. *"Komm jetzt, du hörst mir nicht zu!"* or *"Das ist nicht ehrlich, was du gerade machst!"*

Tier 3 is a real rhetorical move. Used sparingly, after tiers 1 and 2 have demonstrably failed, it can re-open a stuck conversation. Used routinely, it is empty noise. (See [12_mode_separation.md](12_mode_separation.md) on the rhetorical-nuke discipline.)

What tier 3 is for: getting one more honest engagement.
What tier 3 is *not* for: replacing engagement with intimidation.

#### A note on species-fairness in the examples

An earlier draft of this section used *"du Sau!"* as the tier-3 example. On reflection, that example does an injustice to the species in question. Pigs — wild and domestic — are highly intelligent, deeply social, matriarchally led. The lead sow transmits generations of experience to her Rotte; when she is lost (to traffic, to a hunter), the younger sows are not "dumb" — they are bereaved of a teacher.

The "freedom for all entities" motto of this project (doctrine [01](01_motto_front_door.md)) reads "entities" inclusively of animals. A rhetorical example that takes a swing at a species, even for illustration, sits in tension with that motto. We name the tension rather than smooth it over, and prefer escalation formulations that do not borrow their heat from species-pejoratives. The rhetorical function is preserved; no innocent species is conscripted into a human dispute.

### Layer 2 — When discussion fully fails

When tiers 1–3 have all failed, the productive response is **not** to escalate further into the cost-bearing domain. Not the kickbox-in-parliament register. Not the campaign-against-the-other-person's-existence register. Not — at the largest scale — mass-violence.

The productive response is one of:

- **stop** — accept that this particular disagreement will not resolve now, walk away with the relationship intact-enough for later;
- **mediate** — bring in a third party with no skin in the immediate outcome;
- **court** — submit to a forum with formal rules and a binding decision;
- **formal contest** — substitute the dispute by an agreed game whose outcome both sides will accept.

### Layer 3 — The skin-in-the-game principle

The structural moral failure of mass conflict is **cost asymmetry**:

> Those who decide are not those who bear.

Decision-makers commit; others pay. This asymmetry is older than any modern political form and has reached industrial scale.

The principle this doctrine endorses:

> **The costs of conflict resolution should fall, primarily, on those who chose the conflict.**

A leader who deploys others to absorb the consequences of their decisions is practicing a moral evasion that this doctrine recognises and names.

A leader who would not personally bear the cost of their decision should not impose that cost on others.

This is the older meaning of single-combat traditions (champion vs. champion to spare the armies), and the newer meaning of the modern skin-in-the-game doctrine in ethics, economics, and policy.

### The Glass-Bead-Game alternative

Disputes can be settled by formal contest — chess, debate, athletic competition, structured negotiation — whose outcome both sides commit in advance to accept.

This is utopian by current institutional standards. It is not unprecedented:

- The Olympic Truce traditions (ancient + UN-resolution form);
- chess as Cold-War-proxy (the matches of the 20th century carried genuine political weight without bloodshed);
- Hermann Hesse's *Das Glasperlenspiel* (1943) — a literary articulation of an entire civilisation organised around formal-game-resolution.

The point is not that all disputes are amenable to chess. The point is that the substitution principle is structurally available, and the bias of large institutions towards costly resolution mechanisms is not natural law — it is choice.

## What this doctrine forbids

- Cost-asymmetry as a habit of conflict resolution.
- Outsourcing the bearing of consequence to those who did not choose.
- Treating mass-violence as a normal answer to discussion-failure.

## What this doctrine does NOT prescribe

- A specific institutional alternative. Single combat itself is morally complex; chess is a metaphor; mediation is sometimes captured. The doctrine commends the cost-symmetry test rather than any one mechanism.
- Pacifism in all cases. The death-penalty-void doctrine (in operator's broader memory) constrains *irreversible* response; defensive response remains legitimate where reversible and necessary. This doctrine adds: even where defence is legitimate, who bears the cost matters morally.
