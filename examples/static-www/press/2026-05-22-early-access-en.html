vectoryz launches Early Access: Open-Source AI with documented self-auditing

vectoryz, a new open-source AI system, has entered its Early Access phase. Developed by Nuremberg-based IT specialist Benjamin Resch, the project combines locally hosted open-source language models with a verification wrapper that classifies answers at the level of individual claims. The specific models in use are documented in the project repository and may be adjusted as the project evolves.

Per-claim Faktampel marking

At the core of vectoryz is a claim-level reliability indicator called the Faktampel. Individual statements are marked with tiers such as factfact, quasifact, maybefact, quasinonfact or nullfact, allowing users to see how well-supported specific parts of an answer are.

Tribunal of witnesses (drift-active, optional)

The system also includes an optional multi-witness procedure internally called Tribunal-Witnesses, which can check claims against multiple independent sources. By default the procedure becomes visibly active when the system detects possible drift between a model reservation and a proposed answer. Users can enable it manually for deeper audits, or disable it for lower bandwidth and faster iteration.

Phase timeline (calm-by-default)

A compact phase timeline appears below each answer, documenting processing time across stages such as classification, search, generation, Faktampel and Tribunal checks. More detailed diagnostics are available on demand.

Data minimisation + Bröselfrei

Data minimisation is a further focus. According to the project, vectoryz operates with zero cookies and zero tracking. A built-in browser check (internally called default_prophylactic_debroeslar) reports unexpected storage entries on page load. Server logs and chat data are automatically deleted after seven days. Chat content is encrypted client-side with AES-256-GCM; the key remains exclusively in the user's browser.

The next development stage is planned to keep chat data by default only with the user, with transmission to the server occurring only on explicit sharing of a chat.

Audit-open-door

The source code, project documentation, doctrines, commit logs and technical decisions are published under the MIT licence. Known issues and design decisions are documented publicly as part of the project's audit-open-door commitment.

"vectoryz is intended to show that AI answers can be not only generated, but also checked and documented in a transparent way. The goal is to combine speed, local control and verifiable claim quality." — Benjamin Resch, developer of the project

Status and outlook

The system runs on a server in Germany (Hetzner). vectoryz is in Early Access. Ongoing development focuses on improving answer speed while preserving high claim-level accuracy (target: over 98% correct-across-all-statements).
